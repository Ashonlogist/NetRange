Get access

Two separate paths, depending on what you need. Neither involves payment — this page is about what the data is and who can have it.

1. Add coverage reporting to your own site

Self-serve · free

If you run a network, a venue, a hotspot or a campus WiFi, you can add a one-time consent widget to your own pages. Visitors who tap Allow contribute their connection quality to the public map, and you get a dashboard for the data coming from your domain.

  • Register an account and add your domain — no email verification, no billing.
  • Copy one <script> tag into your page.
  • Visitors see a consent prompt once per visit. Nothing is collected unless they tap Allow.
  • Your dashboard shows only data tied to your own registered domain.
Register a site I already have an account
What a visitor is asked for: approximate location (via the browser's own permission prompt, and only if they allow it), their connection type, and the time of the visit. No cookie, no fingerprint, no advertising identifier, and no client-side speed test. The widget asks on every page load, because the promise in the prompt is "once per visit".

2. Need coverage or carrier data at scale

Talk to us

For telcos, ISPs, regulators and researchers who need coverage cells, carrier comparisons or historical trends at volume. This path is deliberately not self-serve: bulk access is a conversation about what you need it for, and the answers are not always the same shape.

What's available

  • K-anonymized coverage cells. Averaged signal and speed per geographic cell, with a contributor count and a recency confidence score.
  • Carrier comparison. Per-network signal distributions, same suppression rule.
  • Daily quality trends. Signal and speed over time per cell, with a minimum-contributor floor per day.
What "safe to share" means here. Every cell is withheld entirely unless at least {{ k }} independent contributors reported from it. This is suppression, not rounding or blurring: a thin cell is not shown in a coarser form, because a coarsened cell is still a cell someone lives in. Small-n breakdowns (demographics, satisfaction) are the specific case this rule exists for, and they are held to the same floor as everything else.
